Description & Commentary:

Rock Exotica's 2010 technical notice describes the BiWire carabiner this way:

The Bi-Wire is a unique design that features an outer gate. This can provide more security than a regular non-locking carabiner (but note that it is NOT a substitute for a regular locking

carabiner). Once you are used to it, it is easy to open and removes as easily as a regular non-locker.

The BiWire is the first climbing carabiner with multiple gates for added security. By compared to Grivel's modern multi-gate carabiners, the design is rather crude. The straight wire gates are more difficult to operate than the curved gates common on Grivels. The gate can easily catch on objects as the nose lacks any guard, and the wires protrude almost an entire wire diameter from either side of the nose.

The Rock Exotica rockD body shape works well as a general-purpose work horse carabiner, but generally considered heavy for recreational tasks, limiting the number brought or limited to disciplines which need few carabiners. Generally, large carabiners are well suited to work environments as gear is only carried short distances, few carabiners are needed and often the user is wearing gloves. At the time of introduction, there were far fewer "low security" carabiners on the market and many users did not know how to adpot this carabiner into their climbing.

The design was not widely used in the rock climbing world, although the large size and dual wires did find favor amongst ice climbers for the same reason multi-gates are common in the discipline today - they can be opened with gloved hands more easily than other secure carabiner designs, and the gates resist ice and debris.

This example is relatively early in the production run, from late 2008 and marked Pat. Pending. The patent was filed in 2007. Laser etched markings use filled/solid characters, and there is no markings on the spine as found on later examples from 2010 and 2013. The carabiner lacks a marking indicating a gate-face rating, but the 2010 technical notice lists a 4kN rating. I see no reason this 2008 example would fair any worse than the later examples.
